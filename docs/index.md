# Toolkit for HPC High School Outreach

A starting point for research computing staff who want to bring high-performance computing to high school students.

## Welcome

If you work in research computing and would like to introduce high school students to high-performance computing (HPC), this toolkit is for you. It was designed for research computing units at universities, including teams with limited staffing and funding.

Several research computing groups have delivered HPC outreach activities for K-12 students, such as workshops and summer camps, and some have shared their experience in conference presentations. However, we have not found a toolkit that helps new organizers get started. We hope this toolkit gives you a practical starting point.

Our project focuses on high school students. Many of them are beginning to think about their future careers, and this is a good time to introduce topics they can explore after secondary education.

## Where should I start?

1. **Read the Start Small checklist.** It walks you through the foundational items for a first program with limited budget and staffing. [View Page &raquo;](./checklists/start-small.md)
2. **Choose an activity type.** Pick the checklist in the site navigation that fits your program: class presentation, half-day workshop, multi-day camp (students home at night), or multi-day camp (students on campus at night). 
3. **Plan your timeline.** Our sample planning calendar shows what to work on in the 12 months before your program starts. *(link coming soon)*

**Note:** Policies differ by campus. We recommend planning early and contacting offices on your campus for specific details.

## What are HPC outreach activities?

For this toolkit, HPC outreach activities are educational programs designed and delivered by university research computing units in the US. Their goal is to introduce high school students to topics in HPC. They may include in-class presentations, half-day workshops, and multi-day camps, among others.

## Why organize HPC outreach activities?

Universities are well-equipped to introduce students to foundational HPC topics, help them build transferable skills, and create a lifelong interest in STEM. We believe these programs offer several benefits.

- They support a university’s mission of public service and sharing knowledge with its state, the nation, and the world.
- They help build a talent pipeline in research computing. For some students, an HPC outreach activity may be their first exposure to research computing as a career.
- They show a commitment to local and regional communities, and they may help universities attract prospective students and employees.
- They help research computing staff build connections with campus offices and the local community.

## How did we build this toolkit?

We interviewed 15 people, including university STEAM outreach program leaders, research computing center staff, K-12 teachers, and specialists in youth protection and in supporting students with disabilities. Their experience shaped every checklist.
